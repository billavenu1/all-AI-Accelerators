Rack infrastructure

Power & liquid cooling

Per rack · 167 kW envelope

Why it's special: a Maia 300 rack is designed for about 167 kW, roughly 2.6 times Maia 200's 64 kW rack PRESS (derived). Liquid removes most of the heat and air handles the rest, so the rack fits water-cooled and air-cooled data centres.

Key numbers

167 kWrack power envelope
>80%of heat removed by liquid
50 Vbusbar down the rack
Dualpower feeds, plus an automatic transfer switch

What it does here

  • Liquid cools the accelerators, voltage regulators and switch chips. Air cools memory, storage and other parts.
  • Connects to facility water, or uses a heat-exchanger unit where the building is air-cooled.
  • Rack and row power readings guide where jobs are placed.
  • Weight and coolant flow are not published.

Compute tray

×24 per rack · 4 accelerators each

Why it's special: each tray carries its own host processor, memory, storage and cooling, so Maia 300 has no separate host trays. That frees space for 96 accelerators per rack, double Maia 200. The two accelerators in a pair link directly inside the tray.

Key numbers

1,324 PFFP4 per rack (derived: 96 × 13.8)PRESS

What it does here

  • Puts four accelerators next to their host, so one board is one serviceable unit.
  • Uses a 1U tray format, down from 2U on Maia 200 PRESS.
  • Per tray (derived): about 1.15 TB of HBM4 and about 55 PF of FP4.

Scale-up network

Scale-up switch trays

×3 trays · 6 switch chips

Why it's special: instead of a proprietary link, Maia 300 uses Ethernet-based networking with Microsoft's own AI transport on top. The network controller sits on the accelerator, so chips talk to each other without a discrete scale-out NIC.

Key numbers

3liquid-cooled switch trays
6high-radix switch chips
3.2 TB/sper accelerator, bidirectional (32 × 400 Gb/s)PRESS
+14%scale-up bandwidth vs Maia 200

What it does here

  • All links inside the rack are electrical (copper).
  • Three distances: a direct pair, the same rack (one switch hop), or another rack (three hops).
  • Lets prefill and decode run on different accelerators and pass the KV cache directly.
  • Switch-chip vendor and per-chip speed are not named.

Concept

Rack power & liquid cooling

A modern AI rack draws as much power as well over a hundred homes, and nearly all of it becomes heat. Fans alone cannot remove that.

Direct liquid cooling pumps coolant through plates on the chips. A busbar is a thick metal rail that carries power down the rack. A heat-exchanger unit lets a liquid-cooled rack work in a building with no chilled-water supply.

Analogy: a car engine. Air cooling is enough for a lawnmower; a racing engine needs a radiator.
Concept

What is a compute tray?

A tray is a flat drawer that slides into the rack. It holds a few accelerators, their host CPU and memory on one board.

Racks are built from identical trays, so a faulty one can be swapped quickly.

Analogy: books on a shelf. The rack is the shelf and each tray is a book.
Concept

What is a scale-up network?

Big models are split across many chips that must swap partial results constantly. A scale-up network links chips with very high bandwidth and low delay, so they act like one big chip.

Maia uses Ethernet, the everyday networking standard, with Microsoft's own AI transport layer on top.

Analogy: people in one open-plan room who can just talk, versus emailing another office.
Concept

What is an AI accelerator (ASIC)?

An ASIC is a chip designed for one job. Maia is built for AI inference: running a trained model to answer requests.

Next to the maths units sits HBM, stacked memory that holds the model. Reading it is often the limit on speed.

Analogy: a GPU is a Swiss Army knife; an ASIC is a tool made for exactly one job.
Concept

What is a host CPU?

Accelerators cannot run an operating system. A host CPU boots the machine, loads data and launches work onto the accelerators.

Analogy: the head chef who reads the orders and hands tasks to the line cooks.
Concept

What is the second network tier?

Inside a rack, chips connect through switches in the rack. To link many racks, Maia 300 adds a second tier of optical switches that carry light over fibre.

Microsoft still calls the whole thing one scale-up fabric.

Analogy: streets inside a city (tier one) and the motorway between cities (tier two).

Why it's special: Maia 300 is the first chiplet-based Maia. Compute sits on 2 nm-class N2P silicon, while memory control and I/O sit on separate dies. Maia 100 and 200 were single dies PRESS.

On-die SRAM

Software-managed scratchpad · size not disclosed

Why it's special: Maia has no automatic cache here. Software decides what sits in SRAM and when it moves, so data arrives just in time. Maia 200 had 272 MB; Microsoft has not said how much Maia 300 has.

8 stacks · 288 GB

Why it's special: bandwidth per watt. 18 TB/s is about 2.5 times Maia 200's, and Microsoft says HBM bandwidth per watt roughly doubles. Memory is split into regions (NUMA), no longer one shared pool as on Maia 200.

Data movement

Data-movement engines (DMA)

Per tile and per cluster

Why it's special: Maia moves data with dedicated hardware, not the maths units. This is the "software-defined dataflow" idea: software plans every move between SRAM, HBM and the network.

Key numbers

Not publishedengine counts or speeds

What it does

  • Tile and cluster DMA engines shuttle data between SRAM and HBM.
  • Reshaper and synchroniser blocks (added in Maia 200) help data flow from network to tile PRESS.

Concept

What is a chiplet?

A chiplet is a small die combined with others in one package. Small dies are cheaper because a defect ruins less silicon, and each can use the process that suits it.

Analogy: building a house from prefabricated rooms instead of carving one giant block.
Concept

What is on-die SRAM?

SRAM is very fast memory on the chip itself. Most chips use it as an automatic cache. Maia lets software control it directly, as a scratchpad.

Analogy: a cook's chopping board: tiny, but everything on it is within reach.
Concept

What is HBM?

High-bandwidth memory stacks DRAM chips and places them beside the processor, joined by thousands of tiny wires.

It is much faster than normal memory, but costly and limited in size.

Analogy: a multi-storey car park next to the stadium, with a hundred exits instead of one.
Concept

What is a DMA engine?

A DMA (direct memory access) engine is dedicated hardware that copies data from one memory to another without using the maths units.

Analogy: a conveyor belt that restocks the chef's station while the chef keeps cooking.
Concept

What is a host link?

The host link connects the accelerator to the CPU that controls it. PCIe is the standard link used inside most computers.

Analogy: the corridor between the head chef's office and the kitchen.

Microsoft's software stack

AccessMicrosoft Copilot, Microsoft Foundry (model serving), GitHub Copilot, Teams and Azure. Microsoft hasn't announced customer-rentable Maia instances.
ServingMicrosoft hasn't named a serving engine for Maia 300. Its design supports splitting prefill and decode across accelerators, with direct KV-cache transfer.
FrameworksPyTorch: Maia workflows "begin naturally with PyTorch" (Maia 200 SDK post)
CompilerMaia Triton compiler for fast kernel generation, plus a compiler pipeline, profiler and debugger (Maia 200 SDK)
KernelsOptimised kernel library and NPL (Nested Parallel Language), for explicit control of data movement and SRAM placement. A full simulator lets teams test before silicon arrives (Maia 200 SDK)
Maia SDKdocumented for Maia 200; one analyst reports Maia 300 uses itPRESS
AI-assistedkernel tuning reportedPRESS
Dataflowsoftware plans moves between SRAM, HBM and network
Not statedany new 2026 SDK release for Maia 300

Why it matters: Maia has no automatic cache to hide poor data placement, so the compiler and kernels carry more of the speed. Microsoft's SDK post covers Maia 200 only; its Maia 300 posts name no software. Microsoft is also tuning its own models and chips together, so most Maia code is its own.
