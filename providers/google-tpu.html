Scale-up network

ICI: the 3D torus

Direct links between chips · 2× the scale-up bandwidth of Ironwood

Why it's special: there are no switch chips between TPUs. Each chip links straight to its neighbours in a 3D torus, which suits training where chips mostly talk to nearby chips. Google says it doubled interchip bandwidth. It hasn't given the new absolute figure (Ironwood: 1,200 GB/s bidirectional over 6 links), so we don't guess one.

Key numbers

2×scale-up bandwidth vs Ironwood
~2 PBshared HBM across the superpod
9,600chips in one torus

What it does here

  • Lets all 9,600 chips work as one machine. Press describes a "shared coherent memory space" PRESS.
  • Reroutes automatically around faulty links.
  • Longest path in a comparable 3D torus: 16 hops (this is why 8tx uses a different layout).

Optical circuit switches

Reconfigure around failures automatically

Why it's special: mirrors steer light, so the wiring of the superpod can change without touching a cable. When a chip or link fails, the system routes around it. Google's goodput target is above 97% .

  • Builds slices of any size and shape, and swaps in spare chips PRESS.

Accelerator

TPU 8t

Announced 22 Apr 2026 · built for frontier-scale training, designed with Google DeepMind

Why it's special: it trades memory speed for capacity and compute. HBM is 216 GB at 6,528 GB/s, against Ironwood's 192 GiB at 7,380 GB/s: more room, about 11.5% less bandwidth (derived). Native FP4 doubles MXU throughput. Don't set 12.6 PFLOPS FP4 against Ironwood's 4,614 TFLOPS FP8 as if equal: the formats differ . Open "The TPU" tab for the blocks.

Key numbers (per chip)

12.6 PFFP4 peak (FP8/BF16 not published)
216 GBHBM
6,528 GB/smemory bandwidth
128 MBon-chip SRAM (Vmem)

What it does here

  • Does the matrix maths, with SparseCores for embedding lookups and all-gather-style collectives.
  • TSMC builds it and Broadcom is the design partner PRESS. Process node and power are not published.

Axion host

Google's own Arm-based CPU · host ratio not published

Why it's special: Google designs both the TPU and its host CPU, so the whole stack is its own. The 8tx variant swaps this host for an AMD EPYC for confidential computing; see the variant selector.

What it does here

  • Boots the system, feeds data to the TPUs and runs the non-matrix work.
  • How many CPUs serve each tray hasn't been published.

Scale-out network

Virgo scale-out fabric

Google's dedicated data-centre fabric for TPU 8t

Why it's special: Virgo is flat, two-layer and non-blocking, built from high-radix switches in several independent planes. Google says one fabric links 134,000 TPU 8t chips at up to 47 Pb/s of bisection bandwidth. With JAX and Pathways, more than a million TPUs across sites can form one training cluster.

Key numbers

134,000chips in one fabric

Up to 4×raw network bandwidth vs previous gen

What it does here

  • Joins superpods, and storage, into larger clusters.
  • Storage gets its speed from TPUDirect RDMA, TPUDirect Storage and Managed Lustre 10T .

Scale-up network

Boardfly group

8 boards · 32 chips, derived: 8 × 4 · linked by copper

Why it's special: A 3D torus is cheap but can need many hops. Boardfly links boards in a group fully, so any chip is at most 7 hops from any other in a pod. A 1,024-chip 8×8×16 torus needs up to 16. Google calls that a 56% cut in network diameter.

Key numbers

7 hopsmaximum, chip to chip
16 hopsmaximum in a 1,024-chip 3D torus
−56%network diameter
Up to 50%better latency on communication-heavy work

What it does here

  • Suits mixture-of-experts models, where every chip may need to talk to every other.
  • Cuts inter-chip delays by up to half, Google says.
  • Uses ICI links at 19.2 Tb/s per chip, 2× the previous generation.

Accelerator

TPU 8i

×4 per board · announced 22 April 2026 · for real-time reasoning and agents

Why it's special: it is built for inference, not training. It has 288 GB of HBM and a large 384 MB of on-chip SRAM, so the KV cache of a long-context model can stay on the silicon. It drops Ironwood's SparseCores for a new engine that speeds up chip-to-chip collectives. Open "The TPU" tab to explore the blocks.

Key numbers (per chip)

10.1 PFFP4 peak (FP8 not published)
288 GBHBM (Rubin: 288 GB; MI455X: 432 GB)
8,601 GB/sHBM bandwidth
384 MBSRAM (3× Ironwood)

What it does here

  • Runs inference: token generation, long chains of thought and agent loops.
  • Peak figures aren't directly comparable: Nvidia Rubin quotes 50 PF NVFP4 and AMD MI455X 40 PF MXFP4, and the FP4 formats differ.
  • Process node and power: not published. MediaTek reportedly co-designed it PRESS.
  • Availability: Google said "later this year" in April and gave no date in November; Next Platform expects 2027 PRESS.

Host CPU

Axion host CPU

Google's own Arm-based CPU

Why it's special: agents don't only run models. They call tools, run code and manage state, and that work lands on the CPU. Google says the number of physical CPU hosts per server doubled vs Ironwood, and it uses NUMA isolation. The exact ratio isn't published.

What it does here

  • Boots the system and feeds the TPUs.
  • Runs the non-matrix work around agents.
  • Bare-metal access is offered, and vLLM and SGLang run natively.
  • Core count and CPU–TPU link speed: Google hasn't published them.

Pod network

Optical circuit switches

36 groups joined into one pod

Why it's special: light, not electronics, links the groups. The switches use mirrors to connect fibres directly, so they add little delay and power. Google's deep dive gives both "up to 1,024 active chips" and "up to 1,152" for the pod. 36 groups × 32 chips = 1,152, so the likely reading is 1,152 physical chips with up to 1,024 active.

Key numbers

Borrowed from TPU 8i · replaces 8t's 3D torus

Why it's special: a torus is great for training but can need many hops. Boardfly connects chips so that any two are at most 7 hops apart, where a comparable 3D torus needs up to 16. Security

Confidential compute

"Hardware-level confidential compute" (Google) · built on AMD SEV (AMD)

Why it's special: it aims to protect data and model weights while they are in use, not only at rest. AMD calls this the first cloud TPU system with hardware-enforced confidential computing. Press says the AMD CPU provides the root of trust and encryption accelerators PRESS.

What isn't published

Open questions

  • Whether it is SEV-SNP, and how attestation works.
  • Whether HBM or ICI traffic is encrypted.
  • Google's Confidential VM docs have said TPUs can't attach to Confidential VMs, so how protection extends into the TPU isn't documented yet.

Synchronisation

Collectives Acceleration Engine (CAE)

1 per chip · replaces Ironwood's four SparseCores

Why it's special: TPU 8i has no SparseCores. The CAE takes their place. It handles the reductions and synchronisation that auto-regressive decoding and chain-of-thought repeat at every step, so the TensorCores don't wait. Google says it cuts on-chip collective latency by 5×.

Key numbers

5×lower on-chip collective latency

What it does

  • Offloads sums and waits across the chip's cores.
  • Is programmable through Pallas and Mosaic.

Native PyTorch on TPUs, replacing the older PyTorch/XLA bridge. It adds true eager mode (run line by line, like on a GPU), sends torch.compile through XLA, supports DDP, FSDPv2 and DTensor, and lets each process run different code, which PyTorch/XLA couldn't. Pallas kernels plug straight in.

Why it matters

Concept

What is a TPU?

A Tensor Processing Unit is Google's own AI chip, an ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) built only for the matrix maths of neural networks. It drops the graphics and general-purpose features a GPU carries, spending that silicon on AI work.

You can't buy one: TPUs are only available through Google Cloud, and they also run Google's own Gemini models.

Analogy: a GPU is a Swiss Army knife; a TPU is a chef's knife made for one job.
Concept

What is a TensorCore?

A TPU TensorCore is the main compute engine. Its heart is the matrix multiply unit (MXU), a systolic array: a grid of small multipliers that pass numbers to their neighbours in rhythm, so each value is reused many times without going back to memory.

Analogy: a bucket brigade. Instead of everyone running to the well, buckets pass hand to hand down the line.
Concept

What is a SparseCore?

Some AI work isn't neat matrix maths: it's looking up a few rows scattered across a giant table, as in recommendation models (embeddings) or routing tokens to experts in mixture-of-experts models.

A SparseCore is a small processor on the TPU specialised for these scattered memory lookups and data shuffles.

Analogy: a librarian who fetches a handful of books from all over the building, so the readers don't have to.
Concept

What is HBM?

High-bandwidth memory stacks DRAM chips on top of each other right beside the processor, connected by thousands of tiny wires. It holds the model's weights and working data.

It is fast but expensive and limited in size, and its speed often limits AI more than the maths does.

Analogy: a multi-storey car park next to the stadium, with a hundred exits instead of one.
Concept

What is on-chip SRAM?

SRAM is memory built into the processor die itself. It is many times faster than HBM but takes far more space per byte, so chips carry megabytes of it rather than gigabytes.

For inference, keeping the hottest data (such as the KV cache of active conversations) in SRAM avoids slow trips to HBM.

Analogy: HBM is the pantry; SRAM is the cutting board right in front of you.
Concept

What is ICI?

Inter-chip interconnect is Google's direct chip-to-chip link. Unlike Nvidia's NVLink, which routes through switch chips, ICI wires each TPU straight to its neighbours, so there are no switches between chips in a group.

It is Google's scale-up network: the fast fabric that lets thousands of TPUs work on one model.

Analogy: houses on a street with doors straight into their neighbours, instead of everyone going via a central square.
Concept

What is a 3D torus?

A topology is the wiring pattern between chips. In a 3D torus, chips sit in a cube grid and each links to its six neighbours (left, right, up, down, front, back), with the edges wrapping around to the opposite side.

It is cheap and scales to huge sizes, and it suits training, where chips mostly talk to their neighbours.

Analogy: a Rubik's cube where walking off one face brings you back in on the opposite face.
Concept

What is an optical circuit switch?

An optical circuit switch (OCS) steers beams of light with tiny tilting mirrors. It doesn't read the data at all; it simply reconnects fibres, so it uses little power and never needs upgrading for faster links.

Google uses OCS to join racks of TPUs into a pod and to rewire around failed racks in seconds.

Analogy: an old telephone switchboard, where an operator plugs a cable to connect two lines directly.
Concept

What is a host CPU?

TPUs can't run an operating system. A host CPU boots the machine, feeds the TPUs data and runs everything that isn't matrix maths. Google's own host is Axion, an Arm-based CPU.

Analogy: the head chef who reads the orders and hands tasks to the line cooks.
Concept

What is a data-centre network?

Beyond one pod, TPUs talk over the data-centre network (scale-out), the same kind of network that connects ordinary servers but built for far higher bandwidth. It lets several pods train one model together.

Analogy: the motorway between cities, while ICI is the streets inside one city.
Concept

Liquid cooling

AI chips produce more heat than fans can remove. Direct liquid cooling pumps coolant through cold plates pressed onto each chip. Google has been liquid-cooling TPUs since TPU v3 in 2018.

Analogy: a car radiator. Air cooling works for a lawnmower; a racing engine needs liquid.
Concept

What is confidential computing?

Normally data is encrypted on disk and on the network but readable while it is being processed. Confidential computing keeps it encrypted inside hardware-protected memory, even from the cloud operator, and lets the customer verify this remotely (attestation).

Analogy: a locked glovebox in a lab. You can work on the sample, but nobody else in the room can touch it.
Concept

Pods, superpods and slices

A pod (or superpod) is the largest group of TPUs joined by ICI into one machine. Customers usually rent a slice: a piece of a pod sized to their job, from a few chips to thousands.

Analogy: a pod is an apartment building; a slice is the set of flats you lease.
Concept

What is Boardfly?

Boardfly is the wiring pattern Google uses for TPU 8i. Four chips on a board link directly to each other. Eight boards form a group, also linked by copper. Thirty-six groups join through optical circuit switches.

The aim is fewer hops. Any chip reaches any other in at most 7 hops, against 16 in a 1,024-chip 3D torus. Fewer hops means less waiting in traffic where every chip talks to every other, such as mixture-of-experts models.

Analogy: a torus is a grid of streets where you cross many junctions. Boardfly adds express routes between districts, so you cross fewer.
Concept

What is a Collectives Acceleration Engine?

A collective is an operation where many chips combine or share results, for example adding up partial answers from every chip (a reduction) and waiting until all are done (synchronisation). The CAE is a small block that does this work in hardware, so the TensorCores don't stall.

Text generation repeats this step for every new word, so the delay adds up.

Analogy: a meeting secretary who collects everyone's numbers and announces the total, so the experts keep working.
Concept

What is a KV cache?

While a model writes an answer, it keeps notes on everything said so far: the key-value (KV) cache. It grows with context length and is read again for every new word.

If the notes are in fast on-chip SRAM, the chip waits less. If they spill to HBM, speed depends on memory bandwidth.

Analogy: a reader's margin notes. Notes on the desk are quick to check; notes in the archive are not.
Concept

What is a board (tray)?

A board or tray is one flat unit that slides into a rack, carrying a few chips and their links. Racks are built from identical trays, so one can be swapped without touching the rest.

Analogy: a drawer in a filing cabinet. Pull one out; the others stay put.
Concept

What is goodput?

Goodput is the share of time a training job spends making real progress. Time lost to failures, restarts, and waiting for a repair does not count.

In a cluster with thousands of chips, something is always failing. Goodput says how well the system hides that. Google's target for TPU 8t is above 97%.

Analogy: a delivery van's "useful driving" time. Traffic jams and breakdowns are not goodput.
Concept

What is FP4, and why can't I compare it?

FP4 stores each number in 4 bits instead of 8 (FP8) or 16 (BF16). Smaller numbers mean more maths per second and less memory, at the cost of precision.

A peak FP4 figure is therefore about twice a peak FP8 figure on the same hardware. TPU 8t's headline is FP4. Ironwood's headline is FP8. Putting them side by side is not like-for-like.

Analogy: measuring a runner in half-steps instead of whole steps. The count doubles, but the distance does not.
Concept

TPUDirect and fast storage

RDMA (remote direct memory access) lets one machine read another's memory, or a storage system's data, without the CPU copying it along the way. TPUDirect RDMA and TPUDirect Storage are Google's versions for TPUs. Google says they help give 10× faster storage access, together with Managed Lustre 10T.

This matters because chips sit idle when training data arrives late.

Analogy: a conveyor belt straight from the warehouse to the workbench, with no one carrying boxes by hand.
