AWS: Trainium3 (Trn3 UltraServer)

Amazon's own AI chip, designed in-house and rented through EC2. Trn3 UltraServers became generally available on 2 December 2025 and join up to 144 Trainium3 chips into one system. Amazon Bedrock already runs production traffic on them.

Power & liquid cooling

The 144-chip UltraServer is liquid-cooled

Why it's special: TechCrunch's lab tour reports that the Trainium3 prototype was air-cooled, like earlier generations, but the production chip is liquid-cooled in a closed loop. Reportedly the coolant touches only the hottest parts: the Trainium3 modules, the PCIe switch and the Graviton CPU. Fans still cool the rest PRESS.

What it does here

  • Removes the heat of 144 chips so they can sit close together and stay in one fast fabric.
  • A smaller 32-chip-per-rack option is reportedly air-cooled PRESS.
  • AWS gives efficiency ratios (4× performance per watt vs Trn2), not watts.

NeuronSwitch-v1

All-to-all fabric · linked by NeuronLink-v4

Why it's special: every chip can reach every other chip through switches, instead of through neighbours. Trn2 wired its chips as a torus, where data hops chip to chip. AWS says NeuronSwitch doubles chip-to-chip bandwidth compared with Trn2 UltraServers.

Key numbers

2.56 TB/sNeuronLink-v4 per chip
2×chip-to-chip bandwidth vs Trn2 (AWS claim)
10switch trays per rackPRESS

What it does here

  • One switch level connects the 4 chips in a blade; two more switches link the blades (AWS Neuron docs).
  • Reportedly built from PCIe Gen6 switch chips PRESS.
  • AWS's pages differ on link speed: 2.56 TB/s on one, 2,048 GiB/s on another, "2 TB/s" on a third. We show 2.56 TB/s.

Accelerator

Trainium3

×144 per UltraServer · AWS's first 3nm AI chip

Why it's special: it is AWS's first 3nm AI chip. Per chip it is 1.5× Trn2's memory and 1.7× its bandwidth. One honest limit: FP4 is not faster than FP8 here, because the chip converts MXFP4 to MXFP8 before the maths. Rubin (288 GB HBM4) and MI455X (432 GB HBM4) hold far more per chip; formats differ, so compare with care. Open "The chip" tab to explore the blocks.

Key numbers (per chip)

144 GiBHBM3e
4.9 TB/smemory bandwidth
2.52 PFMXFP8 / MXFP4 (dense)
671 TFBF16 / FP16 / TF32

What it does here

  • Runs training and inference maths on 8 NeuronCores.
  • Also lists 183 TFLOPS of FP32 and structured-sparsity modes.
  • AWS hasn't published its power draw.

Concept

Liquid cooling vs air cooling

Almost all the power an AI chip uses becomes heat. Air cooling blows air over metal fins. Liquid cooling pumps coolant through a cold plate on the chip, which carries far more heat away in less space.

Denser racks need liquid, because chips packed close together make too much heat for fans.

Analogy: a desk fan versus a car radiator.
Concept

What is a compute blade?

A blade (AWS also says "server" or "sled") is a flat unit that slides into the rack. It carries a few accelerators, a host CPU and network cards on one board.

Racks are built from identical blades, so a faulty one can be swapped alone.

Analogy: books on a shelf. You can pull one out without moving the rest.
Concept

What is a scale-up network?

Big models are split across many chips that must swap partial results constantly. A scale-up network links the chips in one system with very high bandwidth so they act like one big chip.

A switch lets every chip reach every other directly. The older torus design connects each chip only to its neighbours, so data hops along.

Analogy: a central station where any platform connects to any other, versus a ring road where you pass through every stop.
Concept

What is an AI ASIC?

An ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) is a chip built for one job. Trainium does AI maths and little else, so it spends no silicon on graphics or general-purpose features.

The trade-off: software must be written or compiled for it, here with AWS's Neuron SDK.

Analogy: a pasta machine. It makes one thing very well, where a GPU is a full kitchen.
Concept

What is a host CPU?

Accelerators can't run an operating system on their own. A host CPU boots the machine, loads data, launches work onto the accelerators and handles everything that isn't matrix maths.

Graviton is AWS's own CPU family, built on Arm designs.

Analogy: the head chef who reads the orders and hands tasks to the line cooks.
Concept

What is Nitro (a DPU)?

A data processing unit is a network card with its own processor. It takes over the data centre's "plumbing": networking, storage access, encryption and keeping customers apart.

Nitro is AWS's own DPU family. It lets the main CPU and the accelerators spend their time on the customer's work.

Analogy: a building's security desk and mailroom. Residents never handle deliveries or visitors themselves.
Concept

What is scale-out (EFA)?

Scale-out networks connect separate systems so thousands of them can work on one model. It is slower than the links inside one UltraServer, but it reaches much further.

EFA (Elastic Fabric Adapter) is AWS's network interface for this. It moves data between machines with low delay, without going through the operating system.

Analogy: the motorway between cities, while scale-up is the streets inside one city.
Concept

UltraServer vs UltraCluster

An UltraServer is AWS's name for one scale-up system: up to 144 Trainium3 chips behind one set of switches, sold as a single unit.

An UltraCluster is many UltraServers joined by the EFA network inside one data-centre network.

Analogy: an UltraServer is one office floor. An UltraCluster is the whole campus.

Start with the NeuronCores, where the maths happens.

Compute

NeuronCore-v4

×8 per chip · each core is a complete small processor with 4 engines

Why it's special: instead of one big grid of identical units, each NeuronCore combines four different engines, each suited to a different kind of work. They run independently and software decides how to use them.

Key numbers (per core, dense)

315 TFMXFP8 / MXFP4
79 TFBF16 / FP16 / TF32
20 TFFP32
8cores = 2.52 PF per chip

The four engines

  • Tensor: a systolic array for big matrix multiplies and convolutions. It is where almost all the FLOPS come from.
  • Vector: works on rows of numbers where each result needs many inputs, such as layer normalisation. 1.2 TFLOPS FP32.
  • Scalar: one input in, one result out, such as activation functions. 1.2 TFLOPS FP32.
  • GPSIMD: eight general-purpose 512-bit processors that run C/C++, for custom operations.

On-chip memory

On-chip SRAM (SBUF and PSUM)

32 MiB SBUF + 2 MiB PSUM per NeuronCore

Why it's special: this memory is managed by software, not by hardware. The compiler or a kernel writer decides exactly what sits in it and when, so no cache logic guesses wrongly. That control is what the NKI kernel language gives programmers.

Key numbers

32 MiBSBUF per core (up from 28 MiB)
256 MiBSBUF per chip
2 MiBPSUM per core (accumulator)

What it does

  • Holds tiles of data close to the engines, so they rarely wait for HBM.
  • PSUM collects partial sums from the Tensor engine.
  • New in v4: DMA can add into SRAM in a single transfer.

Memory

HBM3e memory

4 stacks · 144 GiB total (36 GiB per stack, derived)

Why it's special: Trainium3 gets 1.5× Trn2's capacity and 1.7× its bandwidth. For scale: Nvidia's Rubin has 288 GB of HBM4 and AMD's MI455X has 432 GB, so each of those holds roughly 2× to 3× as much per chip. Trainium3 makes up some of the gap by joining 144 chips in one fabric.

Key numbers

144 GiBcapacity
4.9 TB/sbandwidth
4stacks

What it does

  • Holds model weights and the KV cache for every request in flight.
  • One AWS page lists 4.7 TB/s instead of 4.9; we use the figure on AWS's main pages.

Data movement

DMA engines and collective cores

128 DMA engines · CC-Cores for collectives

Why it's special: moving data is half the job. DMA engines copy data between HBM, SRAM and other chips without using the compute engines. Separate collective cores run the "all chips combine their results" steps, so that communication overlaps with maths.

Key numbers

128DMA engines
4.9 TB/sDMA bandwidth, with inline compute
16 or 20CC-Cores (AWS pages disagree)

What it does

  • Shapes traffic into 4 classes of service.
  • Can add data into SRAM while copying it.
  • AWS doesn't document a host (PCIe) or security block in the pages we used, so none is drawn.

Concept

What is a NeuronCore?

A NeuronCore is AWS's unit of compute, like an SM on an Nvidia GPU. Each one bundles four engines (Tensor, Vector, Scalar and GPSIMD) with its own on-chip memory.

A systolic array is a grid of multiply units that pass data to their neighbours in a rhythm, which makes matrix maths very efficient.

Analogy: a small kitchen with four stations: one for chopping in bulk, one for sauces, one for single ingredients and one open bench for improvising.
Concept

What is on-chip SRAM?

SRAM is memory built into the chip itself. It is far faster than HBM but takes much more space per byte, so a chip has only tens or hundreds of megabytes.

On Trainium the programmer or compiler places data in it explicitly. It is not an automatic cache.

Analogy: the countertop. Small, but everything you need right now is within reach, where HBM is the pantry down the hall.
Concept

What is HBM?

High-bandwidth memory stacks DRAM chips on top of each other and places them right beside the processor, connected by thousands of tiny wires.

It is much faster than normal memory, but expensive and limited in size, so it is often the real bottleneck for AI.

Analogy: a multi-storey car park next to the stadium, with a hundred exits instead of one.
Concept

What is a DMA engine?

Direct memory access engines copy blocks of data from one place to another on their own. The compute engines keep calculating while the copy happens.

Collectives are group operations, such as "every chip adds up its results and shares the total". Dedicated cores handle them.

Analogy: a forklift crew that restocks the kitchen so the cooks never stop to fetch ingredients.

What's next: Trainium4

Trainium4 is available only inside Amazon. No EC2 instance has been announced, and AWS has published no spec sheet. The public targets, set at re:Invent 2025, are at least 6× FP4 compute, 3× FP8 compute and 4× memory bandwidth versus Trainium3. AWS also said Trainium4 will use Nvidia's NVLink Fusion in MGX racks alongside Graviton and EFA. Delivery is reported for 2027 PRESS.
